## Issue / driver tree (MECE)

`purpose: Compare & decide · when-to-use (trigger): a problem, metric, or claim needs to be decomposed so the audience can see where it comes from and which branch to act on · density: V/B · element: issue-tree · animation: root in (build 1), branches cascade on one press (build 2) · lineage: consulting/recommendation decks (Minto pyramid, MECE); connector geometry borrows the §5.5 static-arrow discipline and the .am-link per-child cascade · build-priority: consulting/data set (after the first teaching wave)`

Reach for the issue tree when a single number or claim ("gross margin slipped four points", "onboarding takes nine weeks", "AI pilots stall before production") needs to be opened up into its mutually-exclusive, collectively-exhaustive parts so a senior audience can see the structure of the problem rather than a list of complaints. The root states the thing under examination; each child reads as a piece of it, and together the children fully account for the root (MECE is the validity test, per the consulting-decks learnings). Use it when the goal is to direct attention to the one branch that carries most of the movement, or to prove that the decomposition is complete before recommending where to dig. Prefer it over a plain bullet list whenever the relationships between parent and parts (sum, summary, drill-down) are the point. Keep it to two to four children at the first level and at most one further level, or it stops being graspable in sixty seconds. It is a framing exhibit, not a data chart: contributions are illustrative or named-benchmark figures, never live financials on the slide.
