## When to reach for it

Reach for this type the first time you put an abstract or technical concept in front of the room and you can feel the audience reaching for a handle. It is the teaching move from [training-decks.md](../learnings/training-decks.md) §3: introduce the unfamiliar by anchoring it to a truly familiar source, map the correspondences one to one, and name where the analogy breaks so the audience does not walk away with the wrong mental model. Use it for "what is a context window," "what is retrieval," "what is an agent," "what is fine-tuning", any moment where a plain definition alone would land as jargon. It is not for comparing two options the audience must choose between (use a before/after or a 2x2), and not for the organizing mental model of the whole talk (use the named-framework figure). Pick one source the room genuinely knows from their own working life, keep it to two or three traits so the mapping stays legible, and always ship the breaks-caption: an unbounded analogy becomes the learner's only, and wrong, model. Density is balanced: two labeled panels, a few short trait chips, and one muted line. The single gold accent rides the correspondence connectors and the target icon, so the eye reads the mapping itself as the point of the slide.
