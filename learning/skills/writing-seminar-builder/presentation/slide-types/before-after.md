# Slide type: Before / after

`purpose: Compare & decide · when-to-use (trigger): make the value of a change vivid, "today vs with-it" · density: B · element(s): before-after (mirrored 2-col) · animation: today (1) to with-change (2) to punch line (3) · lineage: new in the Compare & decide family, shares the mirrored-column discipline of .analogy and the navy/gold encoding of .tri · build-priority: first wave (highest teaching value, was ◻ in _INDEX)`

Reach for **before / after** when the beat's job is to make a change feel worth making, not to define it or rank options. The audience already understands the thing; what they need is to *see the delta*. Two columns carry IDENTICAL row labels down a shared spine, so the eye reads across each row and only the differences pop: left is "Today", quiet navy, the status quo stated plainly and without judgment (house voice: never imply they are doing it wrong); right is "With <the change>", the one gold accent, the same rows transformed. Optional metric-delta chips per row turn a soft "feels better" into a hard "from 3 days to 40 minutes". It is the Compare & decide counterpart to the analogy slide: where analogy maps an idea onto something familiar, before/after maps the present onto the achievable future. Use it once or twice per deck, on a meat beat, after the mechanism is understood and just before the recommendation, so the close has earned its lift. If there is no shared row spine (the two states are not the same things changed), use a two-by-two or a comparison table instead.
