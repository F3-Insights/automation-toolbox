# Slide-type: Next-steps / action plan (Recommend & close)

`purpose: Recommend & close · when-to-use: the close of a recommendation, when the audience has accepted the argument and now needs the concrete Monday-morning moves, each one assignable to an owner with a timing · density: T · element: next-steps · animation: 2 builds (action rows cascade on one press, then the foot-line takeaway) · lineage: reuses .slide-head--assert and .foot-line; borrows the per-child --stagger idiom from .am-links / .rtl-bar so the rows cascade on a single press · build-priority: consulting/data set (after the first wave)`

Reach for this type at the very end of a recommendation arc, right after the recommendation summary, when the job of the beat shifts from "here is what we should do" to "here is who does what, starting Monday." It is the slide that turns agreement into motion: a small set of concrete actions, each with a named owner and a timing, so nobody leaves the room wondering what they personally signed up for. Keep it to four or five rows. More than that and the close stops being a close and becomes a project plan, which belongs in implementation, not the talk. Use the Action, Owner, Timing table when the actions are discrete and assignable; use the 30/60/90 three-column variant when the story is better told as a near, mid, and far horizon than as a list of owners. The single gold accent rides the first action, the one thing that has to happen first. No prices anywhere on this slide: name the next service or step, never a dollar amount. Pair it earlier in the arc with a recommendation summary, and follow it, if at all, only with a send-off.
