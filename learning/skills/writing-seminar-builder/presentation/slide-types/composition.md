# Slide type: Composition (stacked / 100% / pie)

`purpose:` Prove · `when-to-use (trigger):` the message is part-to-whole or mix, "what makes up the 100%", and you want one slice to carry the point · `density:` V · `element(s):` [composition](../elements/composition.md) · `animation:` 2 builds (bar plus its segment labels in, then the gold emphasis plus the takeaway foot-line) · `lineage:` new in the Prove family; shares the bespoke-CSS-bar engine that ranking and waterfall use, the one-focal-series gold discipline of every data slide, and the muted-source line of big-number · `build-priority:` consulting/data set (after the first teaching wave), alongside trend, ranking, and waterfall.

Reach for this type when the so-what is about composition: how a total splits into parts, or how a mix shifts. Prefer the 100% stacked bar over a pie, per the financial-data learnings: a horizontal bar reads its shares left to right, keeps labels off radial wedges, and lets you stack a second bar to compare mix across two groups (this year vs last, segment A vs segment B) on a shared 100% baseline. Use it when one part is the argument, the AI-touched share of spend, the at-risk slice of revenue, the automatable portion of a workflow, and light that one segment gold while the rest stay muted navy. If every part matters equally and none carries the message, a composition chart will read as flat decoration; pick a different beat. If the message is rank-by-size reach for a ranking bar, if it is change-over-time reach for trend, if it is explain-the-delta reach for waterfall. The assertion headline names the so-what of the mix in a full sentence; the gold segment proves it.
