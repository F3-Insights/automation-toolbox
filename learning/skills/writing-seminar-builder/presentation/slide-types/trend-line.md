# Slide type: Trend (line)

`purpose:` Prove · `when-to-use (trigger):` the message is change over a continuous time axis, and one moment in that change carries the point (a turn, a break, a crossover) · `density:` V · `element(s):` [trend-line](../elements/trend-line.md) · `animation:` 3 builds (axes plus comparator from entry, focal line traces in, inflection callout) · `lineage:` new in the Prove family; inherits the `.trace` stroke primitive from `deck-kit/slides.css`, the muted-comparator and data-ink discipline of every data slide, and the single-gold-accent rule · `build-priority:` consulting / data set, after the first wave.

Reach for this type when the story is movement across time and the audience needs to see the shape of that movement, not just two endpoints. It is the line on a monthly cycle-time chart that falls after a pilot goes live, the quarterly adoption curve that bends upward, the DSO trend that finally turns. Pick it over a column chart when the periods are continuous and the slope itself is the message; pick it over big-number when the audience must trust the path, not just the destination. One focal series carries the slide in gold; an optional comparator (a peer benchmark, a flat target line, last year) stays muted navy so the eye reads the focal line against a quiet reference. The single most important move is the annotated inflection: do not make a numerate, skeptical audience hunt for the moment that matters, mark it with a small callout that names cause and magnitude. The assertion headline states the so-what in a full sentence ("Book-close time turned the month we automated reconciliation"); the line proves it; the callout makes it defensible. Every figure carries a named, period-labeled source.
