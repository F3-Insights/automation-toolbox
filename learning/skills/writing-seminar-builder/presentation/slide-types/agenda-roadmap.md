# Slide Type: Agenda / roadmap

`purpose: Orient` · `when-to-use: set the mental scaffold for the talk, and re-anchor the audience when a section begins` · `density: B` · `element: agenda-roadmap` · `lineage: borrows the numbered-node-on-a-rail look from the lifecycle process flow, but it maps the talk's sections, not a workflow with gates; shares the per-child stagger idiom with concept-with-analogy and roadmap-timeline` · `animation: 1 build (the stops cascade in left to right on one press); the "you are here" variant adds no build, the current stop is lit from entry` · `build-priority: first wave`

## When to reach for it

Reach for this right after the title, to hand the audience the shape of the next forty minutes before any teaching starts: three to five stops, each two to four words, on a single path they can hold in their head. An executive who can see the whole route relaxes into the talk, because they know where it is going and roughly how far in they are. Keep each stop a plain section name, not a sentence, and keep it to five stops at most: more and the path stops reading as a scaffold and starts reading as a table of contents.

The same element doubles as a returning section anchor. When you cross into a new section, drop the roadmap back in with the current stop lit gold ("you are here"), so the audience re-locates themselves on the route without you restating the whole agenda. Use the cascade build the first time, when the route is new; on the return appearances let the lit stop carry the orientation from slide entry, with no build, so the re-anchor is a one-beat reset rather than a rebuilt list. This is a setup slide: it carries no leave-behind beyond the route itself, and the substance lives in the sections it points to.
