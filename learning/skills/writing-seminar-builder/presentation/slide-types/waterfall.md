# Slide type: Waterfall / bridge

`purpose: Prove · when-to-use (trigger): explain the delta, "why did the number change" (revenue bridge, cost walk, margin walk) · density: V · element(s): waterfall (bespoke CSS bridge chart) · animation: start+end frame (1) to driver steps cascade left to right (2) · lineage: new in the Prove family; reuses the bar geometry vocabulary and navy/gold encoding shared with ranking and composition, and the data-fades-into-a-visible-axis discipline of the financial-data learnings · build-priority: consulting/data set (was the waterfall ◻ in _INDEX §5 Prove)`

Reach for the **waterfall / bridge** when the beat's job is to explain a change, not to rank items, show a trend, or split a whole. The audience already knows the start figure and the end figure; what they need is the *story between them*: which drivers pushed the number up, which pulled it down, and which one mattered most. This is finance's default explain-the-delta slide, the revenue bridge, the cost walk, the margin walk. A start bar anchors the left, floating step bars cascade across, positive steps in one quiet tone and negative steps in another, with connector ticks carrying the eye from each step to the next, and an end bar anchors the right. Exactly one step lights gold: the driver that carries the message ("the swing was freight, not pricing"). Everything else stays muted navy so the eye lands where the argument is. Use it on a meat beat once or twice per deck, framed by a tight insight headline that states the so-what before the chart is read. If the two endpoints are not the same measure walked through additive drivers (the steps do not sum from start to end), use a trend chart or a ranking bar instead.
