# Slide Type: Section divider

`purpose: Orient` · `when-to-use: mark a topic boundary between two major parts of the talk and reset the audience's attention` · `density: V` · `element: section-divider` · `animation: 1 build (the section title and rule fade in; the progress cue is visible on entry)` · `lineage: shares the full-bleed navy hero treatment and centered .slide-pad of .slide--title and .slide--sendoff, and echoes the agenda/roadmap stop sequence as a quiet progress cue` · `build-priority: orient family, after title hero and agenda`

## When to reach for it

Reach for a section divider at the **seam between two major parts** of a longer talk, the moment where one block of teaching has closed and the next is about to open. Its only job is to reset attention: a near-empty slide, one large section title, and a generous field of space let the room exhale, look up from their notes, and re-orient before the next run of meat slides begins. Use it sparingly, once per major section, never between adjacent slides inside the same idea. It is a setup slide, so it carries no leave-behind artifact and no assertion: a topic title, not a full-sentence takeaway. The optional "stop N of M" progress cue echoes the agenda you set earlier, so the audience always knows where they are in the arc without you having to say it. Choose navy or warm for the mood so the divider reads as a deliberate contrast against the white teaching slides on either side of it. Pick the section divider over the agenda / roadmap type when you only need to announce one boundary, not redraw the whole map; pick agenda when you want to show the full sequence and where the next section sits within it.
