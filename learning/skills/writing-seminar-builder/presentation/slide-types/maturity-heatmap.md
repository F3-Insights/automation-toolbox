# Slide type: Maturity / heatmap assessment

`purpose:` Prove · `when-to-use (trigger):` the audience needs to scan many capability ratings at once and locate themselves on a model, not read one figure or compare two bars · `density:` V · `element(s):` [maturity-heatmap](../elements/maturity-heatmap.md) · `animation:` 2 builds (grid plus row and column labels in from entry, then the rated cells cascade row by row) · `lineage:` new in the Prove family; shares the muted-comparator, one-gold-focal discipline of every data slide and borrows the row-cascade stagger of `.grid-6` and the navy-summary instinct of `.callout-strip` · `build-priority:` consulting and data set (after the first wave), the signature self-assessment visual for an AI-maturity seminar.

Reach for this type when the beat is "here is the whole map of where capability can sit, now find yourself on it." It is the comparison-table cousin built for breadth: capability items down the rows, maturity dimensions across the columns, every cell a Harvey ball or a graduated-tint swatch so the reader takes in dozens of ratings in one sweep rather than mining a numeric grid. It is the natural close of an AI-readiness or AI-maturity arc, because a single highlighted "you are here" row turns a generic model into a mirror the executive can act on. Do not use it to argue a single number (reach for big-number) or to walk a change over time (trend); its job is the at-a-glance scan and the honest self-locate. Keep exactly one row or column gold, the focal "this is the level most mid-market firms actually sit at" or "this is where you are today"; everything else stays quiet navy so the eye lands where the argument lives. The assertion headline states the so-what, the most firms cluster at a developing stage, the readiness gap is concentrated in two dimensions, and the heatmap proves it by showing the pattern, not by listing it.
