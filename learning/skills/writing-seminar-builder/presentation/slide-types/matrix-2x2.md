## 2×2 matrix

`purpose:` Compare & decide · `when-to-use (trigger):` you need to position a set of options on **two** decision variables at once and point to where to start, e.g. effort vs payoff, risk vs reward, reach vs readiness. Use it when a one-dimensional ranking would hide the trade-off the audience actually has to weigh. · `density:` V · `element:` matrix-2x2 · `animation:` axes + four quadrants from entry, plotted items cascade on one press, then the winning quadrant lights gold with a one-line takeaway · `lineage:` Compare & decide family, sibling to Calibration (`.tri`) and Before/after; consulting-deck staple (Minto/MBB), drawn in the deck's data-ink engine, not Mermaid · `build-priority:` first wave of the consulting/data set (the ◻ "2×2 matrix" row in `_INDEX.md` §4).

Reach for it when the story beat is "here are our candidate moves, and they are not equal on two axes." The grid does the prioritization argument visually: the audience reads the spread, the gold quadrant says where to begin, and the takeaway line names the single first move. Keep the plotted set to five or six items so labels never collide; if you have more, the beat is probably a ranking (one axis) or a comparison table (many criteria) instead. The axes must be genuine decision variables the audience controls or cares about, both running low to high in the same direction, so "good" is consistently up and to the right.
