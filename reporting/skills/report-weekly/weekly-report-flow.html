How the weekly report gets made

A leader's admin, built from agents and code. It prepares the leadership-team update; the leader decides what matters, approves it, and sends it.

  • The leader decides
  • An agent judges or writes
  • Code counts, sorts and checks
  • A file handed from one step to the next

The fourteen steps, one line each

  1. 0One time, then quarterly, the leader answers a setup interview that captures seats, audience, categories, direct reports and exclusions.
  2. 1Code collects the week's tasks, mail, meetings and direct reports' reports into one evidence ledger; nothing is judged yet.
  3. 2Code sorts the evidence into the leader's categories, adds items earlier reports left open, and proposes category changes.
  4. 3The continuity agent reads those open items against this week and says which ones the report must answer.
  5. 4The audience editor asks what a leadership-team member would care about, with a reason to keep or leave out each item.
  6. 5Gate 1: the leader confirms this week's categories in one line each: keep, drop, add, move or pull back.
  7. 6Gate 2: the leader fills the silences, answers the batched questions, and pastes in any table worth showing.
  8. 7The writer drafts the one-pager in the house style from the confirmed categories, never inventing a number or a fact.
  9. 8Code verifies the draft: every line traces to evidence, every figure ties to a table, every open item is answered.
  10. 9Gate 3: the leader edits and approves the final text; the size of that edit is tracked as the quality measure.
  11. 10Code renders the approved text and tables into a tight Word file and a PDF, and checks the page count.
  12. 11The email drafter writes the covering note in the leader's voice and places it, with the PDF, in Drafts.
  13. 12The leader reviews the draft email and sends it; the system itself never sends anything.
  14. 13Code records the week and files every published bullet in the report ledger, so next week's report answers this one.
