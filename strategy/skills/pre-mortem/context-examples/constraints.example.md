# Meridian Constraints

*Last revised: 2026-03. Owner: CFO and Engineering Partner.*

The things that bound any strategic decision at Meridian. Pre-mortems should explicitly check the plan against each constraint category and flag any breaches.

## Capital constraints

- **Internal cash + LOC available for a single initiative without partner discussion:** $12M.
- **Above $12M requires:** unanimous partner approval and a board-level financing conversation. Has not happened since 2018.
- **Working capital cushion we will not breach:** $4M minimum on hand at all times. Erosion below this number requires immediate corrective action.
- **Annual capex envelope (maintenance + growth combined):** $4–6M historically. Texas-scale projects sit outside this envelope and are individually approved.

## Talent constraints

- **Engineering bench:** 38 total. Senior tier (10+ yrs at Meridian, food/bev domain): 9 engineers. These 9 are the ones who can run a customer install solo or train a new senior.
- **Spare capacity for non-core projects:** We can spare 1 senior + 2 mid-level engineers for 12 months without backfill, OR up to 5 engineers if we backfill the seniors with external hires (~9 month hiring cycle).
- **Plant manager bench:** Currently 1 (Akron). Hiring an external plant manager has historically taken 6–9 months and 1 in 3 has not worked out within the first 18 months.
- **Operator bench:** Local labor markets vary substantially. Akron operator hiring is reliable. We have no data on labor markets outside Ohio.

## Customer commitments (the load-bearing ones)

- **Three top-10 customers have contracts with Akron-delivery SLAs through end of 2027.** Specifically:
  - Customer A (poultry, 14% of revenue): 8-week install commitment
  - Customer B (beverage, 7% of revenue): Akron-engineered annotation specifically in contract
  - Customer C (poultry, 6% of revenue): penalty clauses tied to Akron production schedule
- **Renewal cycle:** Customer A renews Q4 2026. Customers B and C renew Q1 2027. These renewals are the single largest 2026–27 revenue events.

## Regulatory constraints

- **Multi-state operations expand:** Employment law, sales tax nexus, workers' comp, OSHA reporting, environmental permitting all become per-state. Adding a state ~ adds 6 months of CFO/legal lift.
- **No current Meridian staff in TX:** No legal counsel, no HR, no payroll infrastructure outside OH.
- **Food safety and beverage equipment:** Our installs are subject to FSMA (food) and FDA cGMP-equivalent (beverage) at the customer site. State-level rules layer on for the manufacturing facility itself.
- **Trade compliance:** Some components are sourced internationally. Multi-state operations interact with HTS classification and state-level use tax.

## Cultural constraints

- **Founder-led model.** The CEO is in Akron 5 days/week and has been since 1998. Founder-physical-presence is part of how decisions get made and how customer escalations get handled.
- **Decision-making is collective among 4 partners.** All non-trivial decisions are debated in person. This has not been tested across two geographies.
- **No prior second-facility experience.** Every prior expansion has been within the Akron building or adjacent acquisitions. We have no internal precedent for running a second physical site.

## What we will not do (hard lines)

- We will not enter end-markets outside food processing and beverage bottling.
- We will not subcontract design work to systems integrators.
- We will not take work from any customer where the executive contact has changed twice in the last 24 months: too high a risk of stranded design work.
- We will not take engineering equity (we've been offered).
