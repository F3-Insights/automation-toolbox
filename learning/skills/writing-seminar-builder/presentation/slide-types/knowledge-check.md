# Slide type: Knowledge-check / question

`purpose: Reframe & check` · `when-to-use: attention is flagging mid-session, or a concept just taught needs to be locked in before the next idea builds on it` · `density: T` · `element: knowledge-check (one question, lettered options, adjacent reveal)` · `animation: 2 builds (question with options, then the correct option lights gold beside a why panel)` · `lineage: borrows the lettered-tile treatment from .tri / .two-col surfaces and the navy reveal frame from .def-block; the gold accent rides only the correct option and the why panel; new .knowledge-check block` · `build-priority: second wave (Reframe-and-check, after the first-wave teaching types)`

Reach for this type when the room has been receiving for several minutes and you want them producing instead, or when a concept you just taught is load-bearing for the next stretch and you need to confirm it landed before moving on. Adults learn by retrieving, not absorbing, so a single clear question that forces a guess re-engages the audience and surfaces confusion while it is still cheap to fix. Pose the question, let two to four options sit long enough for a real show of hands, then reveal the answer and the one-line reason it is right, all on the same slide so the feedback lands next to the prompt rather than a click away.

The discipline is spatial contiguity and a positive tone. The answer and the why live adjacent to the question, never deferred to a later slide, because separating a question from its feedback forces the audience to hold both in working memory and the lesson leaks. Keep the wrong options plausible rather than silly, so the retrieval is genuine, and frame the reveal as confirmation rather than a verdict: the audience tested a guess and now sees the model sharpen. One gold accent only, and it belongs on the correct option and the reason beside it.
