## 2023-08-14: brewsystems-acquisition

*(Backfilled retrospectively when this lessons-learned process started in 2025. This entry is the founding example of pre-mortem framing working at Meridian and is referenced in `strategic-history.md`.)*

**Decision:** Killed: culture mismatch on engineering-led vs. sales-led model; M&A would have inverted our identity.

**Predicted kill-shot risks:**
1. Cultural integration risk: engineering-led vs. sales-led operating model collision.
2. Their senior engineering bench was thin (3 named seniors); high flight risk during integration.
3. Their margin trajectory was deteriorating pre-deal in ways the bankers' materials were minimizing.
4. We would have absorbed customer relationships built on a non-Meridian operating posture.

**Outcome at 90 days:** Decision held. They were acquired by a private equity buyer in Q4 2023.

**Outcome at 180 days:** Their operating margin dropped from 11% to 7% (Q1 2024).

**Outcome at 365 days:** Margin further dropped to ~4%. Two of three named senior engineers departed.

**Surprises:** None on the risks we named. We did underestimate how much of their revenue was tied to a single regional account (~22%); that didn't change the kill decision but would have made the acquisition even worse than we modeled.

**Framing that would have caught it:** The pre-mortem caught everything that mattered. Devil's advocate framing was also applied and reinforced the kill.
