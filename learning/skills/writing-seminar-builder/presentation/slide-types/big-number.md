# Slide type: Big-number / stat

`purpose:` Prove · `when-to-use (trigger):` one striking figure is the whole point of the beat, and you want it to land before the audience reads anything else · `density:` V · `element(s):` [big-number](../elements/big-number.md) · `animation:` 1 to 2 builds (figure in, then a short supporting line) · `lineage:` new in the Prove family; shares the centered-hero instinct of `.slide--sendoff` and the muted-source discipline of every data slide · `build-priority:` first wave (high teaching value, no prior element).

Reach for this type when a single number IS the argument and any chart would dilute it. It is the relevance-hook workhorse ("here is the stake before we teach") and the punctuation mark inside a Prove run: one quarter of leaders report a measurable return, adoption doubled in a year, a task that took a day now takes an hour. The figure carries the slide; the context line names what the figure measures, and a small muted source makes it defensible (every number cites a credible, named source, per the universal attribution rule). Use it sparingly. Its power comes from being the only thing on the canvas, so two big-number slides back to back blunt each other. If the point needs two figures to compare, reach for before/after or a ranking bar instead; the optional supporting figure here is a single subordinate stat (a denominator, a prior-year baseline), never a co-equal second protagonist. The assertion headline states the so-what in a full sentence; the number proves it.
