# Slide Type: Value chain

`purpose: Demonstrate · trigger: map activities end-to-end to locate where value or cost concentrates · density: V · element: value-chain (horizontal stages) · animation: blocks cascade L→R (build 1), focal annotation (build 2) · lineage: borrows the gold sf-arrow connector + navy stage treatment from .lifecycle / .shipped-flow · build-priority: consulting/data set (after the first wave)`

## When to reach for it

Reach for the value chain when the beat's job is to lay a **process end to end** in front of the audience and point at the **one stage where the money, the time, or the opportunity lives**. It is the answer to "where in our workflow does this actually pay off?" Unlike the process-flow / stage-gate type (`.lifecycle`), which is about *how work moves through gates and decisions*, the value chain is about *locating value or cost along a known sequence*: same left-to-right reading, but the payload is an annotation or metric pinned to a focal stage, not a HITL gate.

Use it when you have 4 to 6 named activities the audience already recognizes (order to cash, lead to close, intake to discharge, quote to install) and you want to say "the rest is fine, here is the link that moves the number." It pairs naturally after a relevance hook and before a recommendation: the chain shows the terrain, the gold stage shows where to act. Keep it to one focal stage (occasionally two adjacent stages) so the single gold accent stays honest. If you are comparing two options or scoring stages on criteria, use a 2x2 or comparison table instead, the value chain answers *where*, not *which*.
