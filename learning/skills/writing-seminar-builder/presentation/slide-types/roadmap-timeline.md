# Slide-type: Roadmap / phased timeline (Demonstrate)

`purpose: Demonstrate · when-to-use: a multi-workstream plan unfolds across time, and the audience needs to see what runs in parallel, what depends on what, and when the proof points land · density: V · element: roadmap-timeline · animation: 3 builds (header+rows, bars cascade, milestones) · lineage: borrows the gold-CSS-arrow data-URI convention and the per-child stagger idiom from .am-links / .lifecycle · build-priority: consulting/data set (after the first wave)`

Reach for this type when the story beat is "here is the sequenced plan," not "here is the workflow." A process flow (`.lifecycle`) shows one chain of stages with gates; a roadmap shows several **workstreams running in parallel** along a shared time axis, so the value is in the overlap and the milestone timing, not the hand-off logic. Use it for a 30/60/90 adoption plan, a quarter-by-quarter rollout, or a phased capability build where an exec needs to grasp scope and cadence in about sixty seconds. Keep it to 2 to 4 rows: more rows and the bars compress past readability, and the slide stops being a one-glance artifact. The single gold bar marks the workstream the talk is actually about; every other bar stays quiet navy so the eye lands on the focal track first. Pair it earlier in the arc with a plain-definition or framework slide, and later with a next-steps / action-plan slide that turns the picture into owners and dates.
