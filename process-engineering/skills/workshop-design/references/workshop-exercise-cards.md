# Workshop exercise cards

Eight facilitation exercises, each a card with the same five fields so a run-of-show can be assembled from them the way a deck is assembled from slide types. Mechanics are summarised. `workshop-design` picks cards against a goal and a time box.

Card fields: **Name · Duration · Output · Mechanics · Why it works · Best for**

---

**A Day in the Life (process mapping)** · 90 min Output: an hour-by-hour timeline for one role with AI-enhancement opportunities tagged on it. Mechanics: pick two or three roles the room knows; each small group walks one role's real day on a shared timeline; tag every step that is waiting, re-keying, searching, or checking; cluster tags at the end. Why: people describe their own day accurately and someone else's day badly; the tags make the waste visible without anyone saying "waste." Best for: discovery with front-line staff present.

**Information Flow (mapping)** · 60 min Output: a cross-organisation map of where information originates, moves and stalls, with bottlenecks marked. Mechanics: one artifact (a quote, an order, a report) traced from birth to archive across every desk it touches; each hand-off drawn as an edge; edges timed. Why: the artifact is neutral where the org chart is political; a stall shows up as a long edge, not as a person. Best for: any process with more than three hand-offs.

**What If? (scenario generation)** · 45 min Output: a constraint-breaking opportunity list. Mechanics: name the three constraints the room treats as fixed (a system, a rule, a headcount); for each, ask "what would we do if it were gone tomorrow" and capture answers without evaluation; then ask which constraint is actually a choice. Why: it separates constraints that are real from constraints that are habits, and the second kind is where the cheap wins are. Best for: a room that keeps saying "we can't because."

**Opportunity Identification** · 90 min Output: a ranked list of opportunities, each with the pain it removes and who owns it. Mechanics: harvest from the three exercises above plus the pre-read; one card per opportunity; silent placement on impact versus effort; then discussion only where placements disagree. Why: silent placement first stops the loudest voice setting the frame. Best for: the convergence block of a discovery day.

**Implementation Complexity (scoring)** · 60 min Output: a complexity score per opportunity. Mechanics: a weighted rubric of five factors (data readiness, system access, process clarity, change load, dependency count); the room scores each opportunity 1 to 5 per factor; weights are agreed before scoring, not after. Pairs with the impact/effort placement to turn "effort" into something defensible. Why: a number that was argued about is trusted; a gut placement is not. Best for: turning a prioritised list into a sequenced one.

**ROI Calculator** · 60 to 90 min Output: a per-opportunity projection the CFO will accept. Mechanics: three parts, filled in order: implementation costs (build, licences, change), benefit components (hours returned, error cost avoided, cycle time, revenue timing), then projections with the arithmetic shown. Ranges, not points. Why: a benefit with its assumptions visible survives the finance review; a single number does not. Best for: the business case for the top two or three.

**Implementation Roadmap** · 45 min Output: a phased plan with per-phase columns (scope, owner, prerequisites, done-when, date). Mechanics: standard phasing pattern (prove on one workflow, extend to the team, extend to the org); opportunities placed into phases by their complexity score and dependencies; each phase gets one owner. Why: a roadmap with one owner per phase gets executed; one with a committee does not. Best for: the last block of the last day.

**Risk and Change Management** · 45 min Output: a risk matrix (likelihood versus impact, with a mitigation per cell that matters) and a change plan (who is affected, what they lose, what they gain, who tells them, when). Mechanics: the risk matrix is filled by the people who will live with the change, not the sponsor; the change plan names a messenger per affected group. Why: the risks people raise about their own work are the ones that materialise. Best for: any plan that touches how people are measured.

---

Assembling a run of show: total the durations, leave 10 minutes per exercise for transitions, and never schedule two 90-minute exercises back to back. A discovery day is typically Day-in-the-Life or Information Flow, then What If, then Opportunity Identification, with Complexity and ROI on a second day and Roadmap and Risk on a third.
