# Reference model: order to cash

Standard elements, in rough order. Each line is something a business selling goods or services has to handle somewhere; the map should show where, or the gap is a question.

1. **Demand and request.** How requests arrive (channels, formats); who logs them; how a repeat customer differs from a new one; customer and credit set-up for a new account.
2. **Qualification.** Who decides whether to quote; capacity or availability checks; minimum order or margin rules.
3. **Pricing and quote.** Price source (list, contract, cost-plus); discount authority and approval limits; quote validity; quote revisions; who sends it.
4. **Acceptance.** What counts as acceptance (signature, PO, deposit); terms and conditions; prepayment rules; credit check or limit.
5. **Order entry.** Conversion of the quote to an order; order confirmation to the customer; order changes and cancellations after entry; change-order pricing.
6. **Sourcing and fulfilment.** Stock check or purchase from a vendor; vendor confirmation; lead times; partial shipments and back orders; drop-ship.
7. **Logistics.** Freight booking and carrier choice; shipping documents; tracking and the customer's visibility of status; delivery confirmation or proof of delivery.
8. **Exceptions in delivery.** Shortages and damaged goods; returns; replacements; who bears the cost of a return.
9. **Invoicing.** Trigger for the invoice (shipment, delivery, milestone); invoice accuracy checks against the order; freight and tax on the invoice; credit memos.
10. **Collections.** Payment terms; payment methods; cash application; dunning and overdue follow-up; disputes; write-offs.
11. **Reconciliation and reporting.** Billed amounts reconciled to the order and to cost; order and margin reporting; who sees the pipeline and order status.
12. **Records and controls.** Where each document lives; segregation of duties (who can price, approve, invoice and apply cash); audit trail of changes.
