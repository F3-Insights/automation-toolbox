## Recommendation summary

`purpose: Recommend & close · when-to-use (trigger): the analysis is done and the audience now needs the two-to-five things to actually do, stated as the answer up front · density: T · element: recommendation-summary · animation: groups cascade on one press (build 1), governing-thought foot-line on a second press (build 2) · lineage: consulting/recommendation decks (Minto pyramid answer-first, MECE recommendation set); cascade borrows the .am-link / .it-node per-child --stagger discipline; the gold left-rule accent follows .it-root and .relevance-stake · build-priority: Recommend & close set (after the consulting/data wave)`

Reach for the recommendation summary when the work is finished and the slide's only job is to state what to do, grouped, with the answer leading. It is the "answer" section opener in a Pyramid-Principle close: the governing recommendations sit at the top of the slide, each as a verb-led headline ("Stand up a governed tool library", "Consolidate the three intake paths"), with one or two sub-points beneath that say just enough to make the move concrete, and an optional impact tag that names the payoff. Keep the set to three to five groups so it stays MECE and graspable in sixty seconds: mutually exclusive moves, collectively covering the recommendation. Prefer it over a dense paragraph or a flat bullet list whenever the audience needs to leave with a small, ordered set of decisions rather than a wall of findings. It is a closing exhibit, not an action plan with owners and dates, that is the next-steps slide; here the point is the recommendations themselves and the single governing thought that ties them together.
