# Meridian Voice: How We Write Internal Documents

*Last revised: 2025-09. Owner: CEO.*

Internal docs at Meridian are written like engineering memos, not marketing copy. The CFO should be able to forward any internal document to a customer without rewriting it.

## Rules

1. **Plain words.** "The plan" not "the strategic initiative." "Won't work" not "may present challenges." "We're committing $8M" not "we are pursuing a meaningful capital investment."
2. **Numbers first, narrative second.** If a paragraph could open with a number, it should. "$120M revenue, 380 FTE, 88% capacity utilization" earns the next four paragraphs.
3. **Cite the source on any external claim.** URL, document name, or "internal estimate", not "industry says."
4. **Decisions are stated as decisions.** "We are opening a second facility in San Antonio." Not "the direction is to expand into the SW." If the document is recommending, it says "we recommend."
5. **No marketing verbs.** Drop: leverage, unlock, drive, empower, enable, transform. Use: do, build, ship, fix, sell.
6. **No ROI-first framing.** Lead with the operational outcome. ROI follows as a consequence, not a banner.
7. **Past-tense outcomes are honest.** When a prior decision didn't work, say so directly. "Killed at gate 2: culture risk." Not "we revisited the opportunity."

## Length

- Internal memos: ≤2 pages
- Board memos: ≤4 pages
- Pre-mortem briefs: 1 page executive summary + appendices

If a document goes over those budgets, the right move is to cut, not to compress font.

## What we avoid

- Bullet lists when prose would be tighter
- "Going forward" as a phrase
- Italics for emphasis (use bold sparingly)
- Section headers with "the" in them ("The Strategic Opportunity")
- Closing pleasantries on internal docs ("Looking forward to your thoughts")

## The test

If you wouldn't say it out loud in a board meeting with the CFO present, don't write it in an internal document.
