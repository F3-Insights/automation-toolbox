# Slide Type: Relevance hook

`purpose: Orient` · `when-to-use: establish the business stake before teaching a section ("why this matters to you")` · `density: V` · `element: relevance-hook` · `animation: 2 builds (stake statement, then the supporting figure + source)` · `lineage: shares the oversized-numeral treatment with big-number/stat (Prove), but the emphasis is the statement and the stake, not the figure` · `build-priority: first wave`

## When to reach for it

Reach for this at the **top of a section**, before any concept is taught, to answer the only question an executive is actually asking: "why should I spend the next ten minutes on this?" It states one sharp pain or opportunity in a full sentence, then anchors it with a single striking, attributed number so the stake feels real rather than rhetorical. Use it once per section at most. It is a setup slide, so it carries no leave-behind artifact: the figure and the one-line stake are the whole payload, and the depth lives in spoken commentary.

Pick relevance-hook over **big-number / stat** (Prove) when the slide's job is to *open* a section and motivate attention, where the statement leads and the number supports. Pick big-number when the figure itself *is* the argument inside a proof beat. The two share styling on purpose, but they sit in different parts of the arc: relevance-hook orients, big-number proves. Do not stack two numbers here; one figure keeps the stake legible. Frame the stake as an opportunity to capture, never as a failing of the audience (F3 voice: positive and additive).
