# Engagement decks: kickoff, build method, findings readout

Three client-facing decks, one per point in an engagement. Slide-by-slide outlines only; build them as self-contained HTML with the deck kit in the `writing-seminar-builder` skill and red-team them with `executive-red-team` before anyone sees them.

## Kickoff deck (one per workstream)

1. **Project kickoff.** Title, client, workstream, date.
2. **Agenda.**
3. **The consulting team.** A 2x2: name, role, what they do on this engagement, how to reach them.
4. **Engagement focus areas.** Lifted from the SOW. Every workstream shown; the active one boxed, the others greyed. This slide is the scope boundary made visible.
5. **One slide per focus area** (two or three). The SOW language for that area.
6. **What we heard.** Paraphrased themes from the discovery call and the leadership workshop, reframed positively. Unattributed. Positive-additive voice: what could be true, not what is wrong.
7. **Questions to answer before technology.** The decide track. Definitions, ownership, policy questions that no tool resolves.
8. **How the development sprints run.** The build track. Two-week cadence, what a sprint produces, who reviews, how feedback lands.
9. **Our working hypothesis.** The tentative build shape, labelled tentative.
10. **What we target first.** The first sprint's targets, each with an owner and a date.
11. **Interviews.** Who, when, how long, what happens to the recordings.
12. **As-is mapping.** What will be drawn and how it is validated.
13. **Today's data and reporting.** What exists, what the team uses, what is trusted.
14. **Wider team kickoff.** When the rest of the organisation hears about this and from whom (the sponsor, not the consultant).
15. **Known gaps, written down.** What is not yet known, so nobody pretends otherwise.
16. **Discussion.** Three or four cards, one question each.

## Build-method brief (once per engagement, usually with the first kickoff)

1. **The build method.** Title.
2. **Two rules run the whole process.** State them. The ones that held: nothing is built before it is specified in writing; every write to a client system sits behind human approval.
3. **The journey at a glance.** Four phases on one line: Align, Set up, Specify, Build and iterate.
4. **Align.** From first conversation to signed engagement. What happens, what the client gets.
5. **Set up.** Wire up the project before building anything: access, folders, cadence, the register.
6. **Specify.** Interviews in, specification out.
7. **Build and iterate.** Working software early, feedback loops in days.
8. **What this asks of you, and what you get.** One column each. The honest slide.

## Findings readout (to sponsors first, then executives)

1. **Title.** `<Workstream> Project Update`, not "Findings" and not "Results".
2. **Activity since kickoff.** What was done: interviews held, documents read, systems inspected. Counts.
3. **Revisiting prior recommendations.** If earlier advice exists (a previous consultant, an internal review): a table, recommended-then versus happening-now. Neutral.
4. **Findings by category.** One slide per finding, five to seven findings. Each title is a full sentence stating the finding ("Orders are entered once, but delivery dates are retyped in three systems"), never a topic label. Body: about nine unattributed cards of what people said, then the consultant's reading, kept visibly separate from the evidence.
5. **What staff would hand off first.** Their words for the manual work they want gone.
6. **What other firms and researchers say.** External evidence with citations, three or four points. Frames the client's situation as normal, not alarming.
7. **What could be automated or built, with IT considerations.** Options, not a plan. Each with the IT question it raises.
8. **The measurement question.** Where a metric drives behaviour, say so, and offer the alternative in one sentence (e.g. measure on-time delivery by customer order rather than by truck dispatched).
9. **Still open.** What was not learned and why.
10. **Conclusions in the owner's words.** The sponsor's own framing, quoted back, so the readout ends on their agenda rather than the consultant's.

Rules for every deck: mock-ups are referenced ("see the demo mock-up"), never shown before the conversation; every number traces to a source in the claim ledger; the executive red-team runs on the scroll view without notes before delivery.
