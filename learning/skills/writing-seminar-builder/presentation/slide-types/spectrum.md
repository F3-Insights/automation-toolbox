# Slide type: Spectrum / continuum (Explain)

`purpose: Explain · when-to-use: replace a false binary with "where on the dial" · density: V · element: .spectrum bar · animation: bar+poles from entry, markers cascade on one press, you-are-here + takeaway on the next · lineage: first appearance · build-priority: second wave (Explain)`

Reach for the spectrum when the audience is stuck in an either/or that is really a continuum, and the teaching move is to dissolve the binary into a dial. It answers "is it A or B?" with "it is a range, and here is where the realistic positions sit." Use it when two poles are genuinely opposite ends of one axis (manual to fully autonomous, narrow to general, cautious to aggressive) and the value lands by placing 2 to 4 concrete, named positions along that axis so the executive can locate their own situation. The optional gold "you are here" marker is the payoff: it turns an abstract scale into a personal read, which is why this slide earns its place over a plain two-column comparison. Prefer it to a 2x2 when there is one axis, not two; prefer it to before/after when the point is a graded range, not a single jump. Keep it to one axis and one gold accent: the bar and poles are quiet navy structure, and only the you-are-here marker (or the focal position) carries gold.
